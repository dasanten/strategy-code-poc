# Organizations and territory: NPC cities

- **NPC city:** a city run by the game. Self-managed cities are NPC cities. On its own it works
  its farms and workplaces and carries the output to its storage like anyone working a workplace
  (Carrying the output, to the nearest storage of that city; its workers set out with Food for
  the walk and the first days, and the carrier brings the crew's Food back: Crew food), pairs its adults,
  builds a house when it has a food surplus and no free housing, and shares its adults out:
  soldiers by its size first; then farmers, as many as feed everyone (one farmer feeds about
  four) and one more while it has no food surplus; with its Food below its reserve but not yet
  running short, as many more as win back what it lacks in a few game days (a balance number;
  decided overnight, Daniel to confirm), if that is more; building a farm when those it keeps don't
  fit, or when Food is short of its reserve, or with nothing else keeping it from being full of the Food surplus its founding waits on (Full city; decided overnight, Daniel to confirm), with every farm at its full crew (below); then builders while it has a project, at most two in a village and more as it grows (a balance
  number); everyone else is its **spare hands**. Its planning pass gives each free adult the job
  that scores highest (ADR 0029). A worker stays where they are until another job beats theirs by a
  margin, so nobody goes back and forth. Beyond that it is driven by its
  reserve (NPC reserve): its spare hands go to the good shortest of its reserve first; they work
  the workplace that makes the good, or build it first on the nearest fitting resource node
  (within a set reach of its home, or further out where a Scout found one) and work it like its other workplaces, carrying the output to
  its storage (Crew food). With every workplace of a short good at its full crew and someone idle, it opens another, however many it has: on the nearest fitting node for what is dug or cut, in its area for what is processed. Food short with every farm at its full crew opens another farm even with nobody idle: its farmers come from those working for surplus and from its scouts first (Spare hands' work), and from other work only after them. A farm already begun is finished before another, and that new farm comes before anything else it has begun. While Food runs short that farm is the one thing it builds, besides a first farm. So a city builds the whole chain its land allows, one need at a time:
  weapons short means a smithy, which wants Iron, so a smelter, which wants ore, so a mine. A
  good it knows no node of stays short; the city buys it from its trader or a visiting trader,
  or does without. It doesn't begin a building whose materials it has no way to get: no node of
  that good in reach and no trader (Daniel, 2026-11-20, #1487). How the game reads that (Daniel to
  confirm): a material can come if it holds enough, if a finished workplace of its own makes it,
  if it knows a node of it (or, for a processed good, of what goes into it), or if a Trade Org
  warehouse in its trader's reach sells it; a visiting trader or a Player's offer doesn't count,
  as neither is sure to come. A building it began before that rule and can't finish no longer
  comes before its other projects; it waits, begun, until the material can come (Daniel to
  confirm). With nothing short its spare hands still have work (Spare hands' work). When it has no room left inside its wall it grows its area
  by building on the edge, puts up a new wall 8 tiles further out on every side, with the gate in
  the same place, and only once every tile of the new wall is finished tears down the old one.
  Its residents stand on its walls and pass its gates as a realm's do. It keeps some soldiers by
  its size, arms them from its smithy or by buying, fights attackers in its area and closes its
  gates when armed outsiders come near. A Player does the same by hand, if they want to. The world starts out with NPC villages
  and towns, on Plains, far from every starting city and from each other, and out of every
  Player's sight.
- **NPC reserve:** what an NPC city keeps of each good it can use, and never sells: Food for
  some game days for all its residents, and what its stable lacks for its horses (Feeding the
  stable; the stable's Food counts); Wood and Stone for its next house and some wall, more as it grows,
  and while a town builds or upgrades its wall in stone, Stone set aside for that wall (a balance
  number of tiles' worth; Daniel, 2026-11-19, Epic 113): its roads, repairs and trade leave it, its
  founding party's Stone counts only above it, and only its walls spend it, so the building for its
  growing population doesn't use up the Stone its wall needs. It never makes the city short: its
  spare hands refill it working for surplus, so its walls spending it don't pull them off other
  work; weapons
  for the soldiers it keeps by its size, and the Iron and Wood to forge them (a city that knows of a
  coal node wants a steel Sword, Armour and Shield for each soldier instead of a spear, forged in that
  order, and keeps them, their Steel and Wood, the Iron and Coal for that Steel and the Iron ore for
  that Iron: it mines Coal,
  sets every second smelter it has to Steel and forges steel; its soldiers fetch each piece from its
  storage once it holds one and put back what it outdoes. One with no coal node in reach, as in a
  heartland, stays on iron); Coins as its war
  chest, for buying what it lacks and hiring help. A stable is wanted once it has horses, or with
  none once nothing else is short (NPC horses), a mint only with a gold node it knows of. Whatever its storages hold above the reserve is its surplus,
  for sale (Trading with an NPC city); whatever is below is short and drives what it works and
  builds next (NPC city). Coins below the war chest count as short only for a city with a way to
  earn them: gold or a mint of its own (a mint or gold mine, finished or begun, Gold ore in its
  storages, or a gold node it knows of), or trade in reach (a Trade Org warehouse within its NPC
  trader's reach of its home, or a wandering trader whose loop passes close enough to stop at it,
  Trader stop). A Player's offer doesn't count: offers come and go. A city with none of these keeps
  its war chest for everything else (its prices, what it sells and buys, what its traders take
  along, and taking a Player's offer that pays Coins while under it), but it never waits on Coins
  it can't earn.
- **Spare hands' work:** with nothing short of its reserve, an NPC city's spare hands don't stand
  idle. In this order they go on a road crew while it has a road to build (NPC road), scout (Scout),
  and otherwise work at its workplaces for **surplus**, past the reserve, up to a cap per good (a
  balance number that grows with its residents). Once every good they could work for is at its cap,
  they work on past it, the good least far past its cap first, up to a few times the cap (a balance
  number), farms aside, and only those left once its founding party has gathered its materials, so a city with no one to sell to and no room to found stays busy while wear
  and decay use its goods up (decided overnight, Daniel to confirm). With a crew full and its good
  below the cap, a big
  city opens a second workplace of that kind on a node it knows. What is short, its traders and a
  founding party still take them first, and they count as spare hands for a Full city: Food short
  with every farm full opens another farm, and those working for surplus and scouting are its first
  farmers (NPC city). Trade Org
  cities do none of this.
- **Standing idle:** an NPC city's adults with nothing to do, those carrying a child and its children
  stand at a house of the city, not at its storage; a city with no house, at its home.
- **Soldier posts:** between fights an NPC city's soldiers stand spread out: first on its town hall,
  then at its gates and along its wall, each on the post their id gives them among those the city
  has: a balance number on the hall, one at each gate (a balance number too), the rest on the tiles of
  its wall they can get up to. While its wall has too few tiles for them, or it has none, they also
  stand on the free ground around its hall. Once on a stretch of wall they stay, so a new one moves
  nobody. They eat where they stand (Refilling at home), and keep any spare Food on them. When those on the hall or at a gate are called
  away or die, the next by id take their place at the city's next pass. Some of those on its wall (a
  share of them, at most a balance number whatever its size) walk a round along it and its roads
  inside it, and back to where they set out, in turns by id. They refill as they walk, and one low on
  Food with none to take stops and goes back to their post; a stop they can't get to they skip. In a
  city with a training ground some train (Who trains). An alarm takes them from any of this, and they
  come back (Alarm): one on a round goes on with it. Trading posts and the HQ keep their own rules.
- **Bigger projects:** once nothing is short, from materials above its reserve, a big NPC city (by a
  balance number of residents) builds beyond its needs: a training ground, an inner ring of wall
  round its town hall and storage with a gate, and more roads (NPC road). The inner ring goes up tile
  by tile like its outer wall, once that has its gate and isn't moving out, and waits whenever
  something runs short. It takes the shape that leaves fewest gaps, along its layout's streets where
  it can: a square on the lanes round the town hall in Grid, a circle on the first lane in Rings. What
  stands on its line leaves a gap, as in any wall, and a street it crosses stops at it. Its gate
  stands in line with the outer gate and goes up last, so its people never lose the way to the
  storage. The city grows its outer wall as before, and never tears the inner ring down.
- **NPC road:** a road an NPC city builds with a **road crew** of its spare hands (at most a balance
  number at once, which grows with the city, apart from its builders), only from materials above its reserve. Once its full wait is under way (Full city), it also leaves a town hall's and a storage's materials untouched, so founding comes before roads. Its layout's
  streets come first (City layout). Then it follows the
  way a resident would walk, tile by tile, skipping tiles that have a road: first from its home to
  each of its workplaces outside its area, nearest first; then to the nearest Trade Org warehouse
  in its trader's reach, leaving out those of the HQ's inner ring, which keeps everyone out; then to
  the edge of its nearest neighbour city's area; a big city goes on to further warehouses and
  neighbours, nearest first. Like any road nobody owns it, and anyone can use it
  or tear it down; a tile torn down is laid again in time. A way too long (a balance number, in
  tiles) or with no way through is given up. A road two cities would each build between them is
  built by only one. Streets first goes for what the crew starts from the city: one of them out on
  a way, with a tile's materials and the Food for it in hand, lays the tiles nearest them before
  walking home to a street owed meanwhile (decided overnight, Daniel to confirm). The crew takes
  Food along, never starts a tile with too little to walk home
  on, and fetches more by Getting food.
- **Scout:** a spare hand an NPC city sends exploring: a few at a time by its size (a balance
  number) run the game's Explore default with no `find`, and come home when their Food says turn
  back. A new scout is sent only while the Food they set out with comes from above the city's
  reserve, so sending one never makes Food short (Spare hands' work). A city knows every resource node within its set reach (NPC city). What a scout saw is
  the city's only once they step back into its area alive: from then on it knows those nodes, out
  to a wider limit (a balance number), and can build on them. So a good it has no node for is one
  it knows no node of: its trader and a founding go by that too. A scout killed on the way brings
  nothing. Called off for what is short, they come home first with what they saw. Unexplored to a
  scout is what their city doesn't know and they haven't seen. A place a scout or a builder finds no
  way to is given up, and the city no longer counts on the nodes there. It stops scouting once it
  knows its limit: once a scout finds nothing left they can get to, on their Food. Nothing protects
  them on the way.
- **NPC trader:** an NPC city's own trade run. Once a good's surplus makes a full load and a
  Trade Org warehouse (HQ or trading post) lies within a set reach, one of its spare hands,
  never a soldier, carries the load there, sells it, buys the good shortest of the reserve that
  the city has no node for, as far as its coins and their arms allow, and walks home to the
  storage. A village sends one trader at a time, a bigger city more by its size (a balance number),
  each on their own run; they spread over the goods with surplus, and only with no other good to
  sell does one take a second load of a good already on the road. Nothing protects them on the way but what they carry and their escort.
- **Escort** (Epic 97): soldiers an NPC city sends along with its own people on the road. A town's
  trader takes one soldier per run (a village's goes alone); a founding party takes soldiers in
  proportion to its size, at least one, in place of its one armed member. Both take more when the
  city knows the way is dangerous. Only soldiers beyond those on its hall and at its gates go. An
  escort fights for those it guards and keeps pace with them (a trader waits for an escort fallen behind, one gone ahead waits for the trader; #1434); a trader's comes home with them, a
  founding party's stays as the daughter's first soldiers. With no soldier to spare, a trader or
  founding party sets out without an escort.
- **Soldiers in peace** (Epic 97): the soldiers an NPC city keeps by its size are what it keeps in
  peace; it keeps more while it feels threatened. Between fights they stand their posts, walk
  rounds, train where it has a training ground (wearing their gear, Wear) and go as escorts.
  Soldiers just standing on a post count as standing idle. Anyone else of the city armed in peace,
  such as a smith carrying the smithy's weapons to its storage, stands down: they put their
  weapons, armour and Arrows into its storage and go back to work, never those holding its town
  hall; those on a round, escorting, training or on alarm stay soldiers until they are free
  (decided overnight, Daniel to confirm).
- **Full city:** an NPC city that for a whole game week, less the bigger it is (a balance number), has had spare hands (idle, on a road crew, scouting or working for surplus) and nothing
  short of what founding needs, holds a food surplus, and holds its founding party's materials
  above its reserve. What founding needs is Food, Wood and Stone (Daniel, 2026-11-18, #1379):
  its war chest (Coins), and its soldiers' gear and what it is made from (weapons, armour, bows,
  arrows, Steel, Iron, Iron ore, Coal), don't keep it from being full; they kept every city short
  all year. They stay short for all else it does, so it still works and buys them. A city that
  lacks nothing but its founding party's materials counts those as short (Daniel, 2026-11-15,
  T100.8): its spare hands gather them like any shortage, and it builds the workplace for one it
  has none of. Food, Wood and Stone still come first, since only once none of them is short do the
  materials count; Food
  its surplus too (Daniel, 2026-11-15): only once it holds a share of its Food surplus cap past its
  reserve (a balance number); while full, its farmers work to win that back as they do its
  reserve (Daniel, 2026-11-18, #1379). While it saves for its party (its full wait running, no party of its out),
  its walls and repairs spend only the Stone above its reserve and the party's Stone (Daniel,
  2026-11-18, #1379): they kept spending it, so no city held it. Nor does any of its building
  spend the Wood and Stone the party needs (Daniel, 2026-11-19, #1379): a site that needs them
  waits until it holds them above its reserve and the party's, while its farms and the workplaces
  for Wood and Stone go ahead, and meanwhile it builds whatever else it wants that doesn't wait; begun
  workshops took each load its quarry brought home. The same
  holds once its daughter stands, for the Wood and Stone the daughter still lacks of the party's
  load, until its storage stands (#1379: a begun smithy took the Stone its daughter's storage
  needed, and the daughter starved). Its walls still spend the Stone
  its reserve sets aside for them (NPC reserve). Its workplaces come first too (Daniel, 2026-11-16): its spare hands'
  work (Spare hands' work: road crew, scouting, its workplaces for surplus) comes before gathering
  for the party, and only the hands left over gather. Those gathering them are still spare hands
  for its full wait. A dip below the reserve only restarts that time if it lasts longer than a game
  day, though nothing sets out during one. A full city founds a daughter (Founding).
- **Founding:** how a full NPC city spreads out. It picks a free Plains site the usual distance
  from every city (on a map smaller than the game's but at least half its side, scaled with the
  side: Daniel, 2026-11-15, T100.8), within a set reach of itself and on no realm's territory, nearest a node of
  the good it is short of and has no node for. Its party comes from its spare hands and its
  workers near home first, then from its farmers beyond those who feed it (Daniel, 2026-11-18,
  #1379); while too few are at hand, its workers far out are called home to set out, and stand
  there until it goes. A **founding party** of a village's worth of
  adults, more from a big city, one of them armed, each carrying a share of the materials (a town
  hall's and a storage's, and houses for those past a village's worth) and Food for the walk and
  the first weeks, with as many more as it takes to carry it all (Daniel, 2026-11-15, T100.8;
  those bring no house of their own), walks there and builds the town hall, which founds the **daughter** as an
  ordinary NPC city (below). With too few hands to carry it all, the party sets out once it
  holds the town hall's materials and its Food, and the mother sends a **second wave** of
  carriers after it with the rest of the load (Daniel, 2026-11-19, #1438), as hands come free,
  taken like the party: while the town hall is going up they join the party; once the daughter
  stands they take what it still lacks of the party's materials, become its residents and walk
  to it, until its storage stands (from then it gathers what it lacks like any city). Until then
  the daughter's own builders fetch what it lacks from the mother's storage too, with Food for the
  way, and that is no theft (Daniel, 2026-11-19, #1379: a mother with no hands to send left its
  daughter with no storage and so no farm, and its people starved). Like the
  party's extra carriers they stay with the daughter (Daniel, 2026-11-19,
  #1379). A hand called home leaves the workplace far out it
  was called from. One too full of what they carry for their own Food puts down at the storage what crowds it
  out first, so nobody sets out without it (#1379). The daughter is an
  ordinary NPC city with a name from the game; then its storage, a farm and a house. Until its
  storage stands its armed residents build with the rest, since the materials are on them and
  there is nowhere else to put them. A member gone back to the mother's storage for materials
  when the town hall stands comes to the daughter instead. Its first
  wall comes later out of its own reserve, a ring round its home. Mother and daughter are
  independent and hold each other in high standing, which fades like any standing; neither
  shares anything with the other. A city founds again once it is full again. Trading posts and
  the Trade Org HQ never found cities.
